Explaining the residual-reversal project to a quant interviewer

For quantitative researcher, trader and developer interviews at market makers, prop firms and multi-strategy funds. Every number below comes from the frozen or post-audit result files in the repository; nothing here should be said in an interview that cannot be shown in the repo.

1. How to pitch it

Thirty seconds (résumé walk-through)

"I built a point-in-time research system to test whether residual short-term reversal in S&P 500 names still pays after institutional costs when you execute in the closing auction and filter out news-driven moves. I pre-registered the hypotheses, selected on development data, validated once, and ran a single locked holdout on 2023 to 2026. The holdout net Sharpe was 0.16. I then had the code adversarially audited; the audit found that half of my in-sample P&L was an unhedged market exposure caused by a fill-rule bug. I fixed it, reran everything and withdrew the in-sample numbers. The final answer is a research-quality negative result, and the interesting part is what the audit taught me about how backtests lie."

Two minutes (when they say "tell me about the project")

Question. Nagel (2012) and Blitz et al. (2013) show that short-term reversal in liquid stocks behaves like the return to liquidity provision: residual moves not driven by news mean-revert over a few days, and the premium scales with VIX. I asked whether that premium survives today for someone who can execute in the closing auction without paying a spread, once you filter out moves that came with material news.

Design. Point-in-time S&P 500 membership including delisted names; daily and 15-minute bars from 2005; every 8-K acceptance timestamp from EDGAR so the news flag is exact to the second at a 15:40 ET decision cutoff. Signal: z-scored residual return against 250-day market and sector betas, including the partial day to 15:45. Execution: limit-on-close orders that fill only if the official close extends the move, exits market-on-close. Costs from primary fee schedules for two profiles, exchange member and prime-brokered fund, plus Almgren impact on closing-auction volume. Pre-registered hypothesis registry, 140 candidate configurations, deflated Sharpe and CSCV, a one-shot validation window and a holdout frozen with a manifest and run once.

Result. In-sample looked like 0.9; the holdout was 0.16 with a confidence interval from minus one to one and a half. Then the audit: the SPY hedge shared the entry order's limit-on-close condition, so it mostly never filled, the realized book carried about 20% of capital in unhedged market exposure, and about half of the in-sample gross P&L was that exposure times the next day's market return. The development window also violated my own pre-registration by starting in 2005 when intraday coverage only crossed the threshold in late 2013. I fixed the hedge, the eligibility mask, the limit-on-close semantics, wired in the delisting rules, and reran on both windows. On the pre-registered window nothing survives. Classification C.

What I took from it. Two things: a hedge that shares the entry's fill condition is not a hedge, and a window that starts where survivorship and coverage are worst will select the wrong strategy. And the discipline point: the holdout would have been written up as "the edge decayed" if I had not had the code attacked.

Whiteboard version (five boxes)

Universe and data (PIT membership, delisted names, 8-K timestamps) → Signal (residual z-score at 15:45, news and jump masks) → Portfolio (quantile long-short, caps, sector and beta hedge, 10% vol target, gross ≤ 3×) → Execution and costs (LOC fills at the official close, MOC exits, fees, clearing, SEC 31, borrow, Almgren impact) → Evaluation (DEV → one-shot VAL → locked holdout → audit → rerun). Draw the timing line: 15:30–15:45 bar closes, decision 15:45, NYSE cutoff 15:50, fill at the 16:00 print, never used to form the signal.

3. Questions on research design

Why should this alpha exist, and who is paying you?

Temporary liquidity demand: redemptions, index rebalances, dealer hedging, a desk cutting risk. The urgent trader pays a concession to get done; whoever absorbs the flow is paid when the pressure ends. Nagel shows reversal returns co-move with VIX like a liquidity-provision premium. The counterparty is the impatient institution, and the premium exists only when the fast intermediaries are capital-constrained, which is why it concentrates in 2008 and 2020–2021 in my data. The honest corollary: at a one-to-three-day horizon in S&P 500 names, market makers now harvest most of it intraday, so what is left is a stress-regime premium, not a steady edge.

What exactly was pre-registered, and what was not?

H1 to H8 and the fallback family were written before any data pull, with falsification conditions. The 140-candidate grid was defined in code and registered immediately before it ran, and extended once after early earnings-drift results (two more drift variants, logged as STRAT-4 and STRAT-5). Three design changes were made on development data after seeing results and are logged: the impact model corrected from a 14× overestimate, the cost profile moved from retail to institutional at the sponsor's request, and the pre-earnings exclusion switched off. The auditor called those "decisions taken after seeing results" and I agree; the mitigation is that they were all before validation and are all in the registry. I would not claim the grid was pre-registered in the strict sense; the hypotheses were.

Why residual returns rather than raw returns?

Raw reversal is contaminated by market and sector moves that reflect information and do not reverse. Blitz et al. show residualisation roughly doubles the risk-adjusted return. I used rolling 250-day OLS on SPY plus the SPDR sector ETF; 120-day betas were tried first and were too noisy (correlation 0.48 between successive estimates), so the window was lengthened before validation. The residual is z-scored by trailing residual volatility so a 3% surprise in a utility counts more than in a semiconductor name.

Why quantile portfolios and not a regression or ML model?

The question was whether the simple signal has out-of-sample content, not its functional form. Rank portfolios are robust to outliers and need no fitting; the model ladder (ridge, then boosting only if ridge showed repeatable gains on development folds) was registered but never reached because the linear signal failed the holdout. Adding model capacity to a signal with no out-of-sample edge would only inflate the search.

How did you choose the holding period?

From the decay curve, not from a Sharpe search: 6.8 bp on day one and 1.7 bp per day afterwards on the decile spread. The candidates {1, 2, 3} were registered; the book uses overlapping tranches so a three-day hold trades a third of the book each day.

Why the closing auction?

Because the edge is a few basis points per day and the spread would eat it. Auction fills pay no spread, the closing print is deep, and the day-one reversal is captured only if you are in before the close. The alternatives tested: next-open execution forfeits day one; resting limit orders during the next session suffer adverse selection and were rejected on development data; market-on-close is the fallback. The +5 bp slippage kill test is the cleanest statement of the dependence: every window goes negative.

What does the limit-on-close condition do, economically?

A buy fills only if the official close is at least δσ below the 15:45 price, i.e. the stock keeps falling into the auction. That selects entries where the pressure is still active at the close, which is where the day-one bounce is largest. The cost is a 19% fill rate and a book that runs at a quarter of its targeted gross, which is why the annualised returns are small even at a 10% vol target. A post-audit correction: before the fix, cancelled entries were re-submitted as unconditional MOC orders the next day, so the "LOC strategy" was 20% LOC and 37% fallback MOC. Now a cancelled entry stays an LOC order.

4. Questions on data and lookahead

Walk me through the timing at the decision point and prove there is no lookahead.

Signal uses the 15:30–15:45 bar, complete at 15:45; news flags use 8-K acceptance timestamps up to 15:40; betas, residual vols, VIX, the vol-target estimate and the drawdown state are all lagged one day; the daily eligibility panel is used lagged one day with an ex-ante partial-day jump rule on top. Fill is the 16:00 print, which is never an input to the signal. Tests: a perfect-foresight signal earns zero under the engine's lag; past outputs are invariant to perturbing future prices; an after-hours filing flags the next day; perturbing prices between 15:45 and the close leaves eligibility unchanged. The audit found one leak I had missed: the daily jump filter used the full-day residual of day t, excluding about five names a day on end-of-day information. It added about 10% to the validation spread and was neutral-to-negative elsewhere. Fixed and tested.

How is survivorship handled, and how bad is the gap?

Point-in-time membership from the change list, effective the day after the change; prices for delisted names pulled explicitly; delisting returns applied (acquisition or ticker change 0, bankruptcy −100%, unknown −30% after Shumway). 112 of 948 members had no vendor history: 16% of members in 2005, 5.8% in 2013, 1.2% in 2018, none after 2020. The auditor pointed out that the missing names include Bear Stearns, Countrywide and Lehman, exactly the names a reversal book buys on the way down, and that 2008 alone contributed a third of the pre-audit development P&L. That is one of two reasons the pre-registered window starting in late 2013 is the right one. The delisting rules were implemented in the engine but not wired into the research runner until the audit; now they are, with a test.

Are your sector classifications point-in-time?

No, and it matters. Sector labels are today's, and names mapped to XLC or XLRE get no factor return before those ETFs existed, so they are silently ineligible before 2015 and 2018. It is listed as an open item; the fix is a GICS-as-of-date history or SPY-only residualisation before ETF inception.

How do you know the FMP price endpoints are adjusted the way you think?

I verified on Apple's 2020 four-for-one split that the full endpoint is split-adjusted, the non-split-adjusted endpoint is raw and the dividend-adjusted endpoint is total return, then recomputed adjustment factors from the splits and dividends tables with a tolerance check. The intraday series has an inconsistent basis across symbols, so partial-day returns are computed within the intraday series and an ex-ante reliability mask drops names where the intraday and daily closes disagree. The audit still found a dividend mis-scaling: a pre-split cash dividend was subtracted at full size from a split-adjusted intraday price. Fixed by rescaling the dividend with the intraday-to-raw price ratio.

How did you build the news filter, and why 8-Ks?

8-Ks are the one source that is complete, free, point-in-time to the second and available back to the 1990s. Item codes give the category (2.02 earnings, 1.01 material agreement, 5.02 officer change, and so on). An 8-K accepted between the prior decision cutoff and 15:40 flags the stock-day; later filings flag the next day. FMP articles from 2012 are a second tier. Web search cannot be used historically because today's search returns articles written after the fact; it is reserved for the live forward test. The signal science result: earnings-8-K movers continue at about −15 bp per day while no-news movers reverse.

5. Questions on costs, execution and capacity

Itemise your cost model and its sources.

Two institutional profiles. Exchange member: closing-auction fee $0.0008 per share from the exchange schedule, NSCC clearing 2.6e-6 of notional, SEC section 31 at 20.6e-6 and FINRA TAF at $0.000195 per share on sales, general-collateral borrow 0.3%. Prime-brokered fund: $0.0012 per share all-in, same regulatory and clearing fees, no rebates. Impact: Almgren et al. (2005) power law with η 0.142 and β 0.6 on participation in closing-auction volume, proxied from the last bars plus the closing print. No spread on auction fills; a +5 bp per side stress. Total cost runs 0.06 to 0.13 bp of capital per day. The two profiles differ by 0.01 to 0.03 in Sharpe; costs are not what killed the strategy.

Your pre-registered impact model was wrong. What happened?

I registered a square-root model with k = 1 and it produced negative development Sharpes across the board. Checking it against Almgren's published estimates showed it was about 14 times too large. I replaced it with the published parameters before validation and logged the change as a cost correction. The auditor's fair criticism is that the abandonment happened after seeing a negative result; my answer is that the corrected form is the published one, the change is logged, and the kill tests include cost multipliers of 1.5 and 2.

Why do range-based spread estimators not appear?

Corwin–Schultz and Abdi–Ranaldo gave 20 bp or more for large caps, roughly ten times quoted spreads, which Ardia et al. also document. They were rejected on development data and the auction model pays no spread anyway. The honest gap is that I have no NBBO history; the +5 bp stress is the substitute.

What is the capacity?

Net Sharpe falls monotonically with capital in every configuration: for the pre-registered rank selection 0.22 at $1M, 0.07 at $5M, negative from $20M under member costs. The binding cost is impact against closing-auction volume. Two caveats I raise myself: the participation cap is charged as cost but not enforced as a size constraint, so the curve is an extrapolation, and the auction-volume proxy is not the official imbalance feed.

Why are the annualised returns so small if the Sharpe is around one in some cells?

Because the book is under-invested. The volatility target is computed on the targeted book, but only 19% of limit-on-close entries fill, so realized gross runs at 0.1 to 0.5 times capital and realized volatility at 1 to 3%. The report shows three bases: as run, unlevered per unit of gross, and scaled to the 10% target. Scaling would need 0.2 to 2.4 times gross, inside the 3× cap, but impact grows faster than linearly, so the levered figure is an upper bound.

6. Questions on portfolio construction and risk

How is the book neutralised, and what did the audit find?

Dollar-neutral quantile weights, 2% name cap, sector neutralisation, a SPY hedge sized to the ex-ante beta, then a 10% vol target with gross capped at 3× and a drawdown brake that halves exposure at −10% and restores within 5% of the peak. The audit's critical finding: the hedge and the net-exposure correction were computed on the target book and then passed through the same limit-on-close gate as the stock orders, so they filled 98 times out of 560. On days the market fell into the close, buy orders filled and sell orders cancelled, leaving the book long into an oversold close. Realized net-exposure standard deviation was 0.20 of capital against a 0.05 target, and Σ net exposure × next-day SPY explained 53% of cumulative gross P&L. The fix re-sizes the hedge to the filled stock book after the fill decision and executes it market-on-close, then clips dollar-net to ±5% through the hedge. Realized net std is now 0.04 and a test asserts the band.

Why 10% vol and gross of 3×? Where do those come from?

Factor-neutral equity books at multi-strategy funds run 400 to 600% gross; conservative funds around 200%; prime-broker portfolio margin and net-capital haircuts cap overnight equity books near 6×. 3× with a 10% target is deliberately conservative. Sensitivities at 2× and 5× are in every kill-test table and change almost nothing, which tells you the leverage cap is not binding; the fill rate is.

What is the tail risk?

Daily net returns have excess kurtosis of 14 to 70 depending on the cell, single days carry much of the cumulative return (the frozen holdout goes from 0.16 to 0.01 without its best day), and drawdowns are shallow only because realized vol is low. The economic tails are unscheduled news inside the holding window (the pre-earnings exclusion handles the scheduled ones), crowded unwinds like August 2007, and any implementation that pays a spread.

7. Questions on statistics and multiple testing

How many things did you try, and how did you deflate?

Eight hypotheses, 140 grid candidates, about 15 registered diagnostics and about 30 preview runs, none deleted. Deflated Sharpe per family with N equal to the family's candidate count (72 for rank), CSCV with 16 blocks (12,870 combinations) for the probability of backtest overfitting, stationary block bootstrap for intervals. Pre-audit: DSR 0.62, PBO 0.42, already weak. The auditor recomputed DSR at pooled N: 0.51 at 140, 0.38 at 300, and argued that the residualisation and construction ablations should count as trials. I agree the effective N is two to three times what I used. Post-audit, the pre-registered rank selection has DSR 0.19.

Write down the DSR threshold.

SR* = √V[SR] · ((1 − γ) Φ⁻¹(1 − 1/N) + γ Φ⁻¹(1 − 1/(N e))), with γ the Euler–Mascheroni constant, V[SR] the variance of Sharpe across the N trials, all in per-period units; then DSR = PSR(SR*), the probabilistic Sharpe of the selected strategy against that threshold, with the skew and kurtosis correction in the PSR standard error. Simulation tests: PSR of noise is uniform, best-of-N DSR rejects at the nominal rate, PBO ≈ 0.5 on noise.

Your Sharpe standard error: iid or adjusted?

Both reported; the Lo (2002) autocorrelation-adjusted SE is the one used. On the holdout the iid SE is 0.53 annualised and the adjusted SE 0.59, which is where the −1.06 to 1.46 interval comes from together with the bootstrap.

Validation "confirmed" development. Why is that not evidence?

Because the two point estimates had disjoint compositions. Development 0.89 was 1.55 on 2005–2011 and −0.35 on 2012–2018; validation 0.93 was 2.5 and 1.9 in 2020–2021 and negative in 2019 and 2022. Two stress episodes agreeing on a number is not a persistent edge, and my original decision memo said "confirmed almost exactly". That sentence was wrong and was rewritten after the audit.

Your validation gate was net Sharpe greater than zero. Is that a gate?

No. The auditor called it vacuous and was right: an earnings-drift configuration with validation Sharpe 0.03 and an interval from −0.85 to 0.93 "passed" and consumed a holdout run. A real gate should be on the lower confidence bound or on a fraction of the development Sharpe. I did not change it retroactively because the holdout was already spent; it is on the list for the forward test.

8. Questions about the audit and the rerun

Who audited it and how independent was that?

A fresh-context reviewer with a hostile brief, read-only access to the repository and the processed data, and its own verification scripts: 22 findings, 23 numeric checks, every claim anchored to a file and line. I then verified the load-bearing ones independently before changing anything: the era split of the selected candidate, the holdout net-exposure standard deviation, the hedge fill count. A second independent review checked the final report cell by cell against the result files (1,788 comparisons, 30 findings, one blocker: a parameter dropped when re-instantiating the selected earnings configuration). Both reviews are in the repo verbatim.

The auditor classified it D. You say C. Who is right?

Both, for different objects. The auditor's D applies to the positive in-sample claims as originally stated: those were an invalid instrument, and I withdrew them. The C applies to the study after repairs: a clean, reproducible negative with a credible mechanism story. The audit itself says "after the listed repairs the study is a research-quality C". I did not upgrade anything.

After the repairs, the 2005-window event configuration shows 1.13 on 2023–2026. Isn't that an edge?

It is the cell I would most like to be real, and I do not treat it as evidence. It was selected on the window the audit rejected (its development Sharpe is 1.48 on 2005–2011 and 0.30 on 2012–2018), the 2023–2026 window had already been used as the holdout, the number was produced after four simultaneous code changes and examined once alongside five other cells, and the same configuration returned −0.02 on the frozen holdout. Realized gross is 0.25×, +3.9% per year at 3.4% vol. It is a pre-registered forward-test candidate. If an interviewer pushes, the right answer is that a number you looked at once on a spent window is a hypothesis.

What did you not fix, and why?

Point-in-time sectors, enforced participation and position-count caps, permanent-id keying of membership, share counts on raw rather than adjusted prices, vol targeting on the filled book, and the configuration-hash drift between the runner's defaults and the YAML. Each is documented as open. I stopped where further changes would have required a new out-of-sample sample to evaluate, which is the forward test.

9. Questions on results and interpretation

So what did you actually learn about markets?

That no-news residual reversal in large caps existed at a multi-day horizon in 2005–2011 and reappears in stress, that it is a day-one effect, that news movers continue rather than reverse, and that by 2012 the multi-day premium in this universe is close to zero net of institutional costs. The premium migrated to shorter horizons where it is harvested by participants with order-book data and colocated execution. That is consistent with the literature on the disappearance of post-earnings drift in large caps and with Nagel's liquidity-provision interpretation.

What regime hurts it, and what would you monitor live?

Calm, low-VIX markets: net Sharpe 0.52 in the low tercile versus 1.11 in the high tercile on the pre-audit sample, and negative years in 2015–2019 in every configuration. Live, I would monitor the LOC fill rate and its adverse-selection profile, the realized net exposure against the band, trailing twelve-month net Sharpe with a kill rule at zero, and the closing-auction volume of the names I am in.

If you had institutional data, what changes first?

Real NBBO and the auction imbalance feed, which replace the volume proxy and let me size against actual auction liquidity; a point-in-time sector history; a vendor earnings calendar with announcement times; and full delisted-name coverage before 2012. With order-book data the right project moves inside the day, which is a different strategy.

Would you trade it with your own money?

No. Not because of costs, which are tiny for an exchange member, but because nothing that survives the protocol is economically meaningful, and the only interesting cell is not out-of-sample. The forward test is the correct next experiment.

10. Engineering questions

Describe the simulation engine and the C++ component.

A daily engine that reads target weights at decision t and fills at bar t plus lag, requires a printed trade to fill, separates mark price from fill price so auction execution P&L is explicit, applies delisting returns on the delisting date, and charges each cost component from the traded shares and notional. The C++ version is a C-ABI DLL compiled with ziglang and loaded through ctypes, with a file-based parity harness at 1e-9 and a 6.7× speed-up. I chose a DLL over a Python extension because linking a CPython 3.14 extension with a clang toolchain against the MSVC interpreter was unproven on the box.

How do you make a run reproducible?

Every run writes a manifest with the git hash, a hash of the configs and a hash of the data-snapshot manifest; bulk data lives outside the repo under a versioned snapshot; the holdout runner refuses to run twice; the registry is append-only. The auditor reproduced the holdout Sharpe to four decimals from the feature panels. The one reproducibility gap: the frozen holdout was produced by the pre-audit code and feature panels, which are kept separately; current code reproduces the post-audit files.

What was the worst performance problem and how did you fix it?

Each backtest took 533 seconds because the decision-price panel re-read 782 intraday files and parsed timestamps with string formatting. Caching the last-bar-close panel, filtering on integer times and vectorising the sector neutralisation and limit enforcement took it to 7 seconds with identical output, which is what made a 140-candidate grid and a 22-variant kill suite feasible.

What tests would you show me first?

The no-lookahead test (perfect-foresight signal earns nothing under the lag), the hand-computed synthetic P&L test, the halted-then-delisted test, the after-hours 8-K flag test, and the post-audit tests: hedge sized to the filled book and net within band, cancelled LOC entries re-submitted as LOC not MOC, eligibility at 15:45 invariant to the close, dividend rescaling on the Apple split.

11. Traps to avoid

Do not quote 0.89 or 0.93 as results. They are withdrawn. If asked what the in-sample Sharpe was, say what it was and immediately say why it was wrong.

Do not present the 1.13 cell as a finding. Say "forward-test candidate, selected outside the pre-registered window, examined once on a spent sample".

Do not say "leakage-free", "beta-neutral" or "delisting rules applied" without the qualifier "after the audit". The pre-audit documents said all three and the audit showed all three were false at the time.

Do not call the 2023–2026 rerun a holdout. The holdout was the single frozen batch; the rerun is a diagnostic.

Do not overclaim pre-registration of the grid. The hypotheses were pre-registered; the grid was registered in code just before it ran and extended once.
