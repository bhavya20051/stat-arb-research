# Résumé bullets (metrics only from the frozen and post-audit result files; revised 2026-09-06 after the red-team audit)

1. Built a Python/C++ statistical-arbitrage research system (point-in-time S&P 500 with delisted names, 15-minute bars 2005–2026, SEC 8-K event layer timestamped to the second, closing-auction execution model) and ran a pre-registered, locked-holdout test of residual short-term reversal across 140 registered configurations and three strategy variants under two institutional cost profiles; the locked 2023–2026 holdout returned a net Sharpe of 0.16 (gross 0.26) and the project reports a research-quality negative result with full cost, capacity and multiple-testing accounting.

2. Commissioned an independent adversarial audit of my own backtest, confirmed its two critical findings with my own checks (an execution-gated hedge that left ±20% of capital in unhedged market exposure and accounted for about half of the in-sample gross P&L; a development window that violated the pre-registration), repaired the code with six new tests, reran every result, and withdrew the in-sample Sharpe ratios of 0.89/0.93 in the final report; realized net-exposure standard deviation fell from 0.20 to 0.04 of capital.

3. Implemented the research controls a quant desk expects: hypothesis registry with trial-type accounting feeding a Deflated Sharpe Ratio and CSCV probability of backtest overfitting, a 22-variant kill-test suite (cost stress, execution delay, parameter neighbours, placebo, regime, universe and concentration cuts), capacity curves by cost profile ($1M–$200M) from primary-source exchange, clearing and regulatory fee schedules, 48 unit tests including hand-computed P&L, no-lookahead and delisting cases, and a C++ simulation engine (ziglang, ctypes) with 1e-9 parity and a 6.7× speed-up.
